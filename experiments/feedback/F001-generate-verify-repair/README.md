# F001-generate-verify-repair

## Hypothesis
Mechanism of the generate-verify-repair loop, shown with a seeded stub generator on a synthetic verifiable task (no claim about the quality of any model's generations). ptr_feedback::RepairLoop accepts a candidate only if the real VerifierFabric (the ActionContractVerifier plus stub verifiers that answer Unknown, Disputed, shallow or with a made-up hard finding, and a boundary that forges a self-contradicting Pass) passed it at Deterministic level with no hard finding; verifies every candidate exactly once; stays within its verifier-call and round budget; repairs only the slots its critique names (the hard findings of the report) and rewrites whatever it cannot repair (Unknown, Disputed, a contradicting Pass, a Fail without a hard finding, a declined repair); returns Exhausted, never a best-effort failure, when the budget is spent; keeps a trajectory that agrees with the verifier's log; and agrees with Population::best_verified, which applies the same acceptance rule. Task: produce an ActionIr that meets an ActionContract with seven independent defect slots (target, operation, capability, input type, effect, payload size, payload encoding); three profiles of the stub generator's per-slot skill (uniform, heterogeneous, hard); arms single-shot, rewrite-only (a fresh draw per round, the critique ignored) and repair (a draw of only the named slots, each fixed with probability 800 per mille) at verifier-call budgets 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16, 2000 tasks per profile and seed. The oracle is independent of the product: a harness-owned contract check, a call-counting boundary around the fabric, and an exact analytic dynamic program over the 2^7 defect states that gives the distribution of verifier calls and slot draws of each arm. The comparative rules (repair succeeds on at least as many tasks as rewrite-only and rewrite-only as single-shot at every budget, and repair spends no more slot draws per success than rewrite-only) are consequences of the stub's stated skill: they test that the loop uses the verifier's verdicts and critiques, not that any model repairs better than it rewrites.

## Primary metrics
The 36 metrics of `experiment.toml`: the eighteen hard counters (`false_accepts`, `unverified_returns`, `double_verifications`, `budget_violations`, `collateral_edits`, `exhausted_as_success`, `verdict_mismatches`, `verifier_call_mismatches`, `expectation_violations`, `product_panics`, `calls_after_acceptance`, `premature_exhaustions`, `missed_acceptances`, `trajectory_mismatches`, `selection_mismatches`, `repair_gain_shortfalls`, `draws_gain_shortfalls`, `altered_candidates`), the repair gains, the successes, verifier calls, slot draws and draws per success of each arm and budget (integers, pooled over the three profiles), and the coverage counters.

## Baseline
single-shot + rewrite-only

## Falsification
See `experiment.toml` and [criteria.toml](criteria.toml): twenty-six falsifier rules (the eighteen hard counters `<= 0` and eight strict comparative rules, `repair_gain.b<B> >= 1` and `draws_gain.b<B> >= 1`) and twelve coverage rules, every seed must pass. The eight comparative rules are consequences of the stub generator's stated skill: they test that the loop uses the verifier's verdicts and critiques, not that any model repairs better than it rewrites.

## Rule

Scope: mechanism only (owner decision 2026-10-09). The generator and the repairer are seeded stubs with a stated per-slot skill on a synthetic task of seven independent defect slots of an `ActionIr`; the verifier is the real `VerifierFabric` of the real `ActionContractVerifier` and scripted stub verifiers (Unknown, Disputed, shallow, lying) behind a boundary that also forges a self-contradicting Pass. The experiment makes no claim about the quality of any model's generations, and the outcome says nothing about whether repair beats rewriting for a real generator. The negative controls run the harness' own reference loop, not the product (they show the counters can fail; the mutation plan shows a defective product is caught).
